An employee does not need to leave Wire Finance for their access requirements to change.

They might move to another department.

Receive a promotion.

Take on a temporary assignment.

Change manager.

Or gain responsibilities that require completely different systems and information.

This creates one of the easiest ways for unnecessary access to accumulate.

Imagine someone moves from Sales to Operations.

The simplest approach would be:

```text
Existing Sales Access
        +
New Operations Access
```

The employee can now perform their new job.

But they may also still have access to systems and information from a job they no longer perform.

That is exactly what the **Mover process** is designed to prevent.

## The Mover Process

The purpose of the Mover process is to manage changes to an existing employee's identity, role, department, responsibilities, or access requirements.

A role change should not simply add new access.

**It should also remove access that is no longer justified.**

![Wire Finance Mover Process]({{ '/assets/images/active-directory/mover_process.png' | relative_url }})

The workflow begins with the change itself.

HR confirms the employee and effective date.

The current manager helps identify access that may no longer be required.

The new manager confirms the employee's new role and responsibilities.

IT and Identity can then compare the old access model with the new one before permissions are changed.

Security becomes involved where exception or privileged access needs to be reassessed.

The goal is not:

**give the employee more access.**

It is:

**recalculate what their access should now be.**

## What Can Trigger a Mover Process?

A Mover event is not limited to changing departments.

Wire Finance treats several changes as potential reasons to review an identity:

- Department change
- Job role change
- Promotion
- Demotion
- Temporary assignment
- Location change
- Employment-type change
- Manager change
- Responsibility change
- Privilege change

Some of those events may result in major access changes.

Others may result in none.

The important thing is that the change creates a reason to ask:

**Is the employee's existing access still appropriate?**

## Recalculating Effective Access

The access baselines we created earlier become particularly useful during a Mover event.

Instead of manually guessing what the employee should keep and what should be added, Wire Finance can recalculate access around the employee's new position.

![Wire Finance Mover Access Formula]({{ '/assets/images/active-directory/mover_access_formula.png' | relative_url }})

The principle can be expressed as:

```text
New Effective Access

        =

Company Baseline

        +

New Department Baseline

        +

New Role Baseline

        +

Re-approved Exceptions

        -

Obsolete Access
```

Notice the final part:

**minus obsolete access.**

That is what prevents access from simply accumulating throughout someone's career.

## A Simple Example

Consider Noah Williams.

At the moment, Noah is a Sales Representative.

His normal access may look like this:

```text
WF-BASE-001
      +
Sales Department Baseline
      +
Sales Representative Role Baseline
```

Now Noah moves into Operations as an Operations Specialist.

We should not simply add:

```text
Operations Department Baseline
      +
Operations Specialist Role Baseline
```

to everything he already has.

Instead, the old and new access models are compared.

```text
OLD

Company Baseline
      +
Sales Department Baseline
      +
Sales Representative Role Baseline

             ↓

        COMPARE

             ↓

NEW

Company Baseline
      +
Operations Department Baseline
      +
Operations Specialist Role Baseline
```

The company-wide baseline may remain.

The Sales department access may need to go.

Sales role permissions may need to go.

Operations access may need to be added.

And any exception or privileged permissions need to be reviewed rather than automatically carried forward.

## The Access Review Decision

Not every existing permission receives the same treatment.

Wire Finance classifies access during a Mover review according to what should happen next.

| Classification | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Retain** | Still required | Keep |
| **Remove** | No longer justified | Remove |
| **Add** | Required by new role | Add |
| **Review** | Business need uncertain | Obtain approval |
| **Expire** | Temporary access ended | Remove |
| **Reapprove** | Sensitive or privileged access still required | New approval |

This gives the review process more precision than simply asking whether access exists.

For every important entitlement, the question becomes:

```text
Do we keep it?
Remove it?
Add something new?
Review it?
Allow it to expire?
Or require fresh approval?
```

## Exceptions and Privileged Access Do Not Automatically Follow

This is especially important for access that already sits outside the normal baseline.

Suppose Noah previously received an exception because he was supporting a temporary Sales project.

Moving into Operations does not mean that exception should follow him indefinitely.

Likewise, if an employee already has privileged access, a role change should trigger a reassessment of whether those elevated permissions are still justified.

```text
Existing Exception / Privileged Access
             │
             ▼
        Mover Event
             │
             ▼
         Reassess
        /        \
   Still Needed   No Longer Needed
       │                │
       ▼                ▼
   Reapprove          Remove
```

Sensitive access should survive a role change because it is **still justified**, not merely because it already exists.

## Validate the New Access State

Removing old permissions and adding new ones is not the end of the process.

The updated access still needs to be validated.

The employee should have what the new role requires.

Access from the previous role should no longer remain unless there is a legitimate reason.

Any sensitive or privileged access should have the appropriate approval.

And the manager should be able to confirm that the employee is ready to perform the new responsibilities.

Only then can the Mover record be closed.

## Access Should Follow Responsibility

The Mover process gives us an important identity-management principle:

**access should follow the employee's current responsibility, not accumulate from every responsibility they have ever held.**

That means a change in role is not just an opportunity to grant access.

It is also an opportunity to remove it.

And sometimes the biggest identity change is not a promotion or department move.

It is the end of the relationship entirely.

When someone leaves Wire Finance, we need to answer a different question:

**How do we remove their access completely without losing control of the business information and resources they leave behind?**
