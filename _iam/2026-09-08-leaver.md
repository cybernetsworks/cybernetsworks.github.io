Eventually, every identity lifecycle reaches its final stage.

Someone leaves Wire Finance.

That might happen through resignation, retirement, the end of a contract, dismissal, or a higher-risk departure where access needs to end immediately.

At first, the objective seems obvious:

**disable the account.**

But that is only one part of the problem.

What about active sessions?

Privileged identities?

Exception access?

Group memberships?

Application permissions?

Licences?

Shared resources?

Business documents owned by the employee?

A Leaver process therefore has to do more than switch off a user account.

It has to make sure that **access ends while the business remains in control of its information and resources**.

#### The Leaver Process

Wire Finance uses a controlled offboarding workflow to identify and remove the access associated with a departing employee.

![Wire Finance Leaver Process]({{ '/assets/images/active-directory/leaver_process.png' | relative_url }})

The process begins with HR confirming the departure and its effective time.

The manager identifies any business handover requirements.

IT and Identity then review the employee's access, while resource owners determine whether business data or ownership needs to be transferred.

Security becomes involved where privileged access or additional review is required.

The goal is not simply:

**account disabled.**

The real goal is:

**access removed, ownership preserved, and the departure properly closed.**

##### Not Every Departure Is the Same

Wire Finance does not treat every Leaver event identically.

The timing and urgency of access removal depend on the type of departure.

| Leaver Type | Example | Access Removal Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Planned voluntary departure** | Employee resigns with notice | Scheduled for agreed end date and time |
| **End of contract** | Contractor reaches contract end date | Scheduled automatically where possible |
| **Retirement** | Planned employment termination | Scheduled |
| **Involuntary termination** | Employee is dismissed | Coordinated immediate removal |
| **High-risk termination** | Security, fraud, or misconduct concern | Immediate controlled access revocation |
| **Abandonment / No-show** | Employee stops attending work | HR-authorised suspension or removal process |

The difference matters because access removal has to match the risk.

A planned departure gives Wire Finance time to coordinate handover and schedule the account disablement.

A high-risk departure may require access to disappear immediately.

##### Planned and High-Risk Departure Paths

The two broad paths look very different.

![Wire Finance Departure Paths]({{ '/assets/images/active-directory/departure_paths.png' | relative_url }})

For a normal planned departure, the organisation has time to prepare:

```text
Departure Confirmed
      ↓
Business Handover
      ↓
Access Reviewed
      ↓
End Date Reached
      ↓
Access Removed
      ↓
Ownership Completed
```

A high-risk departure changes the priority.

The first concern becomes **containment**.

```text
High-Risk Departure
      ↓
HR + Security Coordination
      ↓
Exact Termination Time
      ↓
Identity Disabled
      ↓
Sessions Revoked
      ↓
Privileged Access Removed
      ↓
Security Review
```

The business handover still matters, but it cannot come at the expense of leaving risky access active.

##### Disabling the Standard Identity Is Not Enough

This is one of the most important parts of the Leaver design.

A person may have more than one form of access.

Their standard identity may be connected to:

- Company-wide access
- Department memberships
- Role memberships
- Application access
- Data and resource permissions
- Shared-resource permissions
- Exception access
- Licence assignments

So disabling the Microsoft Entra user account is important, but the access review has to look beyond the primary identity.

The question is not simply:

**Has the account been disabled?**

It is:

**Has everything associated with the person's access been identified and removed?**

##### Privileged Identities Must Be Checked Separately

If the employee also has a privileged identity, Wire Finance treats it as a separate removal task.

For example:

```text
Standard Identity
      ↓
Disable and remove normal access

Privileged Identity
      ↓
Remove administrative roles
Remove privileged groups
Remove eligible assignments
Remove elevated permissions
```

Privileged removal can include:

- Administrative role assignments
- Privileged access groups
- Eligible privileged assignments
- Security administration permissions
- Application administration
- Azure administrative access
- Other elevated permissions

This is important because disabling the standard account does not automatically prove that every privileged entitlement has been removed.

**The privileged identity should be verified separately.**

##### Exception Access Ends Too

Exception access should not survive the employment relationship simply because it was originally approved.

If the departing employee has access through:

`SG-EXCEPTION-*`

that access should be included in the offboarding review and removed with the rest of their entitlements.

An exception may have been legitimate while the employee worked at Wire Finance.

Once the relationship ends, the justification changes with it.

##### Active Sessions Matter

Account disablement and session revocation are related, but they are not the same thing.

Wire Finance therefore includes the revocation of active access and sessions as part of the Leaver workflow.

The desired state is:

```text
Identity Disabled
      +
Active Sessions Revoked
      +
Groups Removed
      +
Permissions Removed
      +
Privileged Access Removed
```

Only then do we have a much stronger assurance that the former employee can no longer continue using company resources.

##### The Business Data Still Belongs to Wire Finance

Removing access does not mean deleting everything the employee touched.

A departing employee may own or manage resources that the organisation still needs.

That can include:

- Business documents
- Shared documents
- Mailbox information
- Teams or collaboration resources
- SharePoint resources
- Reports
- Application-owned records
- Automated processes
- Shared resources where the employee is an owner

Those resources need to be reviewed before the identity is permanently removed.

Where necessary, ownership should be transferred to another employee, manager, or resource owner.

This gives the Leaver process an important rule:

**employment ends, access ends — but business data remains controlled.**

##### Licences and Entitlements Can Be Recovered

Access removal also gives Wire Finance an opportunity to recover resources that are no longer required.

Licence assignments should be removed so they can be reused or returned to the available pool.

The same applies to group memberships and other entitlements that existed solely because of the employee's role.

Offboarding is therefore not only a security process.

It is also part of maintaining a clean and accurate environment.

##### Validate Before Closing the Record

The Leaver process should not end simply because someone clicked **Disable account**.

Before closure, Wire Finance should be able to confirm that:

- The identity has been disabled at the correct time
- Active sessions have been revoked
- Department and role access has been removed
- Exception access has been removed
- Privileged access has been separately reviewed and removed
- Licences have been recovered
- Required business ownership has been transferred
- Any necessary security review has been completed
- Remaining access has been validated as removed

Only then should the Leaver record be closed.

##### The Lifecycle Is Complete

We started with a new employee entering Wire Finance.

The **Joiner** process created the identity and established the right access.

The **Mover** process changed that access when the employee's responsibilities changed.

And the **Leaver** process removes access when the business relationship ends while preserving the information and resources Wire Finance still needs.

```text
Joiner
   ↓
Give the right access

Mover
   ↓
Keep access aligned

Leaver
   ↓
Remove access and preserve control
```

That completes the identity lifecycle.
